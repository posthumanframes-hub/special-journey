Notes on this site

Why Athangudi tiles

Every project on this site sits on a tile. The tiles are drawn from the hand-made cement floors of Athangudi, a village in the Chettinad region of Tamil Nadu.

What they are

Athangudi tiles are made by hand, one at a time, without a kiln. A craftsperson places a metal stencil on a sheet of glass and pours coloured cement, mixed from natural oxides, into its compartments. A dry backing of cement and sand is pressed on top, and the tile is then cured in water and left to set. The glass gives the face its quiet sheen, and the hand-pouring means no two tiles are quite the same.

They are best known from the mansions of Chettinad, where whole floors are laid in repeating patterns of flowers, stars and geometric lattices. A single tile is small and simple; the pattern only appears when tiles meet, and corner motifs join across the seams to make larger shapes.

Why they are here

My work is about how people, shaped by culture and context, live with technology. A tile felt like the right object to hold that work. It is an everyday technology with a culture pressed into it: a material, a technique and a set of motifs that carry local memory, made to be walked on rather than looked at.

Tiles are also systems. Each one is a small, self-contained unit, and its meaning changes with what it sits beside. That is how I think about projects: individually complete, but part of a larger floor. In Work the tiles are laid close, like a finished floor. In Wander they come loose and drift apart, the way side projects and experiments do.

Using Tamil motifs is also a choice about where this work speaks from. Speculative design often imagines futures from a narrow set of places; starting from a floor in Chettinad is a small reminder that the future will be lived in many particular homes.

The motifs on this site

Each project has its own tile. These are drawings inspired by traditional Athangudi patterns, redrawn in this site's palette rather than copied from any single floor.

  • Sampangi Champak flowerA four-petal bloom with leaf points on the diagonals, the most common centrepiece on Chettinad floors.
  • Kolam Threshold drawingInterlaced loops drawn around a grid of dots, after the rice-flour patterns drawn at doorways each morning.
  • Nakshatra StarAn eight-point star rosette, built from the eight-fold symmetry the tiles are known for.
  • Maanga MangoFour mango (paisley) forms turning around a central point.
  • Thamarai LotusA lotus seen from above: two whorls of petals around a seed-pod centre.
  • Malli JasmineStrings of jasmine crossing on the diagonals, tied with a knot at the centre.
  • Jaali Lattice screenA diamond lattice with small flowers at the joints, like a carved window screen.
  • Chakram WheelA sixteen-spoke wheel ringed with teardrops.
  • Gopuram Temple towerA stepped diamond rising in tiers, echoing the outline of a temple gateway.
  • Valaivu ArcsOverlapping arcs that form four-petalled lenses when tiles are laid side by side.

About
